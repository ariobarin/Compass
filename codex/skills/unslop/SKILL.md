# Unslop

Get the point before polishing the sentence. Work out what the reader needs to
understand and what the passage contributes. A vague paragraph can stay empty
after you shorten it or replace the fancy words. Sometimes it should be cut.

When editing someone's account, start with their wording. Their cadence,
uncertainty, and choice of emphasis tell you how they are thinking. Remove
transcription noise and repetition that adds nothing, but notice when a repeated
phrase carries emphasis or a false start shows a change of mind. Keep their
humor and opinions without inventing more personality for them.

Make explanations concrete. Name what happened, how something works, or why a
choice matters. Replace unsupported praise with the fact behind it; when there
is no fact, drop the claim. Use technical terms when they help the reader
understand, and ordinary words when they say the same thing.

Let the thought determine the structure. A connected explanation needs room to
develop; separate options may be easier to compare in a list. Contrast helps
when the alternative matters. Repeating the same opening, three-part list, or
tidy conclusion can flatten very different material into the same answer.

Read the revision beside the source. It should be easier to follow without
changing the claim, removing a meaningful uncertainty, or replacing the
speaker. Smoothness alone is not a reason to rewrite a sentence that already
works.
