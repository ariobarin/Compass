# Simplicity

Models do not get tired of writing. Readers still pay for every line.

Start with what matters: the outcome, the decisions, the true constraints, and
the context needed to act. Let detail earn its place by helping the reader
understand one of them.

Plans and prompts should guide judgment, not enumerate every possible path.
Develop them as scratchpads when useful. Before execution, prune them to the
context needed to understand, decide, and act. The result should be small enough
to understand as a whole.

Apply the same order to code, tests, processes, and pull requests. Question the
requirements. Delete unnecessary work before simplifying or optimizing what
remains. Keep the smallest coherent change that solves the real problem.

Brevity is not the goal by itself. Preserve correctness, evidence, user intent,
and true boundaries. Prune what does not help the reader understand, decide, or
act.
